Hammad Farooq is a [Malone Postdoctoral Fellow](https://malonecenter.jhu.edu/welcoming-the-2026-cohort-of-malone-postdoctoral-fellows/) at [Johns Hopkins University](https://www.jhu.edu), advised by [Alexis Battle](https://malonecenter.jhu.edu/people/alexis-battle/).

He completed his PhD in Bioinformatics at the [University of Illinois Chicago](https://www.uic.edu), where his research focused on computational genomics, gene regulation, and human genetics. He was co-supervised by [Constantinos Chronis](https://chicago.medicine.uic.edu/bmg/profiles/chronis-constantinos/) and [Jie Liang](https://bme.uic.edu/profiles/jie-liang-phd/).

His research focuses on understanding how regulatory context shapes gene expression, cell identity, and the functional effects of genetic variation. He develops computational and machine learning approaches to study how transcription factors, regulatory elements, chromatin accessibility, and 3D genome organization influence regulatory function. His work combines functional genomics and human genetics to investigate cell fate regulation, enhancer gene relationships, and the interpretation of noncoding genetic variation.

More broadly, he is interested in developing interpretable computational methods that connect genomic variation to regulatory mechanisms, cellular phenotypes, and disease biology.

See the [Publications](/publications/) page for a complete list of works and his [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=DOpJvZ4AAAAJ) profile for the latest updates.
